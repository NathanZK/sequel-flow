# Workflow architecture

Sequel Flow separates creative generation, evaluation, planning, prose, and
revision so each stage has a clear purpose and useful handoff.

## Flow

**Source / Brief → Ideation → Creative Review → Human Selection → Story
Architecture → Human Approval → Drafting → Review → Targeted Revision → Final
Story**

### Ideation

Ideation is a first-class stage because a longer story needs narrative
opportunities, not just more words around the source's existing beats. It
generates alternatives for scenes, characters, conflicts, relationships,
subplots, settings, motifs, and turns, within the selected story mode.

### Creative Review

The Creative Reviewer evaluates proposals for interest, originality,
coherence, character motivation, thematic fit, tone, and compatibility with
the selected mode. It identifies strengths, risks, and alternatives; it does
not decide canon or approve proposals on the human's behalf.

### Human selection and story architecture

The human selects among reviewed possibilities and decides meaningful
adaptations. Story Architecture then connects the selected material into
character arcs, causal threads, turning points, and a scene sequence. Chapter
allocation follows this architecture: chapters are containers for a story,
not the starting point for inventing one.

The human approves the architecture before prose drafting. Approval focuses
on consequential choices, such as the story's direction, major character
changes, and resolutions of source ambiguity—not routine creative details.

### Drafting, review, and revision

The drafter writes from the approved architecture and current continuity
context. Review evaluates the prose for narrative interest, clarity,
characterization, pacing, coherence, and consistency with the approved plan
and story mode. A targeted revision addresses actionable findings without
requiring a new approval artifact for every prose adjustment. Significant
changes to the story's direction return to the human for approval.

## Creative status and continuity

The workflow distinguishes:

- **Source canon:** what the source explicitly establishes.
- **Approved adaptation:** a deliberate, human-approved change or
  interpretation of source material.
- **Novel-only invention:** material created for the new story that is not
  asserted to be source canon.
- **Unresolved ambiguity:** source material intentionally left undecided.

Source and continuity checks belong in creative review, drafting context, and
story review. The relevant source facts, approved adaptations, inventions, and
open ambiguities should travel with the ideation options, architecture, and
draft as needed. Checks should be proportional to risk and mode; they should
not become a separate exhaustive validation system.

## Why there is no dedicated Validator

A separate Validator would often repeat the reviewer's continuity and
source-fidelity checks while adding another gate and handoff. Instead, review
includes focused source and continuity checks when the selected mode requires
them. A distinct check can be requested for a specific high-risk question, but
it is not a mandatory role or universal approval stage.

## Stage handoffs

Each handoff should carry only what the next stage needs:

1. **Source / Brief → Ideation:** source material or brief, chosen mode,
   intended audience and scope, known canon, and unresolved ambiguities.
2. **Ideation → Creative Review:** distinct story possibilities, each with its
   narrative purpose, mode fit, major assumptions, and likely tradeoffs.
3. **Creative Review → Human:** strengths, concerns, compatibility assessment,
   and useful alternatives.
4. **Human Selection → Story Architecture:** selected ideas, rejected
   directions where relevant, and explicit decisions on consequential
   adaptations.
5. **Architecture → Drafting:** story premise, arcs and threads, scene
   sequence, intended emotional movement, continuity context, and open
   questions.
6. **Drafting → Review:** prose plus the approved architecture and context
   needed to judge it.
7. **Review → Revision:** prioritized, concrete findings tied to the prose and
   architecture.
8. **Revision → Final Story:** revised prose and a concise account of
   unresolved review concerns or material changes requiring human attention.

Keep working documents only when they improve creative decisions, continuity,
or collaboration. Git history is sufficient for ordinary change history.
