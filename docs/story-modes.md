# Story modes

Choose a mode before developing ideas. It sets the source's authority and the
meaning of fidelity; it is a creative contract, not a technical schema.

## `EXPANSION`

- **Source authority:** The source's established events, characters, and
  world facts are canon. Unresolved ambiguities remain open unless the human
  approves an interpretation.
- **New material:** New scenes, characters, conflicts, relationships,
  subplots, settings, and motifs may substantially develop the story, provided
  they fit established canon and do not silently replace its events.
- **Human approval:** Required for changes to established events or facts,
  major character outcomes, and resolutions of protected ambiguity.
- **Fidelity:** Preserve the source's core story and established canon while
  making a fuller, dramatically developed work from it.

## `SEQUEL`

- **Source authority:** The source is authoritative for the history and
  established facts of the earlier story.
- **New material:** New events, conflicts, characters, relationships, and
  consequences may drive the continuation.
- **Human approval:** Required for significant reinterpretations of prior
  events, reversals of established outcomes, and decisions to resolve
  important ambiguity from the source.
- **Fidelity:** Honor the source as prior history while making a coherent new
  story that follows from it.

## `PREQUEL`

- **Source authority:** The source is authoritative for the later events,
  established facts, and outcomes that the prequel must lead toward.
- **New material:** Earlier events, relationships, conflicts, and causes may
  be created, including material that explains how characters or situations
  reached the source's opening.
- **Human approval:** Required when a proposed cause constrains or changes a
  major source ambiguity, conflicts with later canon, or predetermines a
  consequential character interpretation.
- **Fidelity:** Make the earlier story compatible with the source's later
  events without claiming invented explanations were present in the source.

## `COMPANION`

- **Source authority:** The source establishes shared-world facts and any
  relevant timeline or setting constraints. Its plot need not be retold.
- **New material:** A new cast, plot, conflict, and point of view may explore
  other parts of the same universe.
- **Human approval:** Required for changes to shared-world canon, direct
  alterations to source events, or resolutions of important ambiguity that
  affect the shared universe.
- **Fidelity:** Remain compatible with the source's relevant world facts while
  allowing the companion story its own identity and narrative focus.

## `INSPIRED`

- **Source authority:** The source is a reference for explicitly selected
  themes, concepts, motifs, or stylistic characteristics; its plot and canon
  are not automatically binding.
- **New material:** A substantially different story may use new characters,
  settings, conflicts, relationships, and outcomes.
- **Human approval:** Required to confirm which source qualities are intended
  to carry forward and to approve any chosen direct use of source characters,
  settings, or plot.
- **Fidelity:** Honor the selected influences in spirit or craft, not
  continuity with the source. Judge it as a new story rather than by source
  event coverage.
